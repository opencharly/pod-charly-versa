**7 of 7 byte-identical.** That is the statement this leg exists to make: the move of a skill's
prose into the file its repo owns does not change the artifact the corpus publishes — not for these
seven skills, and by construction not for any consumer of them. It is also why no family outside
`versa` is in scope and why the diff's only other entries are the two prose surfaces above.

**One byte, and the verb's asymmetry behind it — filed, not worked around.** The extract verb's
entry writer emits `skillFrontmatter(...) + s.Content` verbatim (`extract.go:126`) while its own
reference writer two lines below (`extract.go:132`) and the generator's emitter
(`emit_skills.go:31`) both write `strings.TrimSuffix(content, "\n") + "\n"`. On this repo the entity
`content:` arrives without a trailing newline, so all seven extracted files lacked one, and each
differed from the published projection by that single byte — and by nothing else:

```
$ tail -c 3 <the extracted file> | xxd | tail -1
00000000: 7965 72                                  yer     ← no 0x0a
$ tail -c 3 <the published projection> | xxd | tail -1
00000000: 6572 0a                                 er.     ← 0x0a
```

The corpus never drifts from it — the emitter re-adds the newline, so `TrimSuffix(body,"\n")+"\n"`
lands the same bytes either way — but the two verb outputs disagreeing at EOF breaks the
`extract → read → emit == the committed corpus` round trip the migration rests on, and it makes the
authored-vs-published diff above the check that would show it. The seven files therefore carry the
emitter's shape (exactly one trailing newline), and the verb's asymmetry is filed:
`opencharly/plugin-marketplace#33`, with the mechanism, the 7-of-7 measurement and the suggested fix.
